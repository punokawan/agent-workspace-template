# International Go-to-Market

How to choose, prepare and enter a new country or language market. Use it when a plan includes
market expansion, or when the product context lists target markets the business doesn't serve yet.
The product context file records languages, locales and markets in priority order (see
**product-marketing**, Languages & Markets); this reference turns that list into an entry plan.

No market is a default. Rank markets from the business's own evidence, not from a generic list of
"top markets".

## Prioritize Markets

Score each candidate market on evidence you have:

| Factor | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Existing demand | Inbound leads, signups, traffic and paying customers already coming from the market |
| Fit | The ICP exists there in numbers; the problem is as painful; the alternatives are similar or weaker |
| Access | Channels that reach the ICP, partners, a language the team can sell and support in |
| Cost to serve | Translation, legal and tax work, payment methods, support hours, sales coverage |
| Competition | Local incumbents, and whether your positioning holds against them |
| Size | Addressable customers or revenue (see [market-sizing.md](market-sizing.md)) |

Enter markets one or two at a time, in order of score. Markets that share a language or a regulatory
area (one translation, one compliance setup) can enter together.

## Readiness Gate

Enter a market only when all are true:

- [ ] Demand signal: inbound leads or signups from the market in the last 90 days
- [ ] Product works there: language, formats, integrations and payment methods the market needs
- [ ] Legal and compliance requirements met (data protection, consumer law, tax registration)
- [ ] Pricing localized: currency, taxes, and price points checked against local alternatives
- [ ] Sales coverage decided (see Entry Phases), if the motion needs sales
- [ ] Support coverage during local business hours, in the language customers use
- [ ] Budget and an owner assigned

## Market Profile

Write one profile per market before entry. Fill it from customer interviews, local partners and
deal data; label anything that is a generalization still to be checked.

| Factor | What to record |
|---|---|
| Buying cycle | Typical length from first touch to purchase |
| Decision process | Individual buyer or committee; who signs |
| Price sensitivity | What buyers compare price against; preferred billing terms |
| Communication | Formality, form of address, directness, what proof persuades |
| Relationship | How much trust must be built before purchase; role of references and partners |
| Compliance expectations | Certifications, data residency, contract terms buyers ask for |
| Channel mix | Channels that reach the ICP here, with expected cost per lead from your own tests |
| Messaging | Which value themes lead; local proof (customers, logos, reviews from the market) |

Record the language and tone decisions (formal or informal address, terms that stay untranslated)
in the product context file's Languages & Markets section so every skill applies them.

## Entry Phases

**Foundation (first 1-3 months):**

1. Local presence that buyers check: prices in local currency, local legal pages, a local contact
   method where it matters to buyers.
2. Sales coverage, if the motion needs it. Options, from lowest to highest commitment:
   - Remote team working the market's hours
   - Local agency or reseller (lower fixed cost, shared margin, less control)
   - Local hire who is a native speaker of the market's language
3. Localized core pages and materials: homepage, pricing, top product pages, sales deck, onboarding.
4. First local proof: case studies or quotes from customers in the market, even beta users.
5. Paid and search tests on high-intent terms in the market's language to learn costs and messaging.

**Growth (months 3-12):**

1. Scale the channels with the best cost per acquisition in that market.
2. Build local partners: integrations, resellers, associations, communities.
3. Local events and content in the market's language.
4. Certifications or compliance work that buyers there ask for.

Set a validation goal per phase in advance, for example a number of paying customers or a pipeline
amount from the market within 90 days, and decide what happens if it is missed (extend, change
approach, or pause).

## Localization Checklist

**Website**
- [ ] Translated by a native speaker (machine draft plus native review at minimum)
- [ ] Prices and checkout in local currency, with taxes handled
- [ ] Legal pages (privacy, terms) in the local language where required
- [ ] hreflang and locale URLs set up (see **seo-audit** and **site-architecture**)

**Product**
- [ ] UI translated, if the market needs it
- [ ] Date, time, number, address and currency formats
- [ ] Room in the layout for longer translated text; right-to-left support if needed

**Payment**
- [ ] Local payment methods buyers expect
- [ ] Invoices in the format and language local rules require

**Legal**
- [ ] Data protection law of the market
- [ ] Consumer and contract law (cancellation, auto-renewal, refunds)
- [ ] Tax registration where required

**Sales and support**
- [ ] Localized deck, one-pagers and objection answers (see **sales-enablement**)
- [ ] Local references
- [ ] Support hours and languages; localized help content

**Marketing**
- [ ] Campaigns written in the market's language, not only translated
- [ ] Keyword research redone in the market's language (see **seo-audit**, **ads**, **aso**)
- [ ] Local social and community presence, if the ICP is there

**Before launch:** a native speaker reviews every localized asset.

## Measuring Each Market

Track per market, not only in total: traffic, signups or leads, conversion rate, CAC, sales cycle,
win rate, churn and support load. A blended number hides a market that costs more than it returns.
Review each market at the end of every phase against its validation goal.
