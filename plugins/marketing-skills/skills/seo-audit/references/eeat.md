# E-E-A-T Checklist

Use this when an audit reaches content quality, when rankings dropped after a core update, or when the site covers YMYL topics. E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) comes from Google's Search Quality Rater Guidelines. Raters don't rank pages; the guidelines describe what Google's systems try to reward, so treat this as a quality lens, not a score.

## Trust comes first

Trust is the most important of the four. Google's guidelines say an untrustworthy page has low E-E-A-T no matter how experienced, expert, or authoritative it seems. A page loses trust through misleading claims, hidden ownership, or inaccurate information, whatever its credentials.

## Checklist by signal

### Experience
- [ ] First-hand experience is shown, not only research summaries ("we tested," "I used")
- [ ] Original photos, screenshots, or data instead of stock images
- [ ] Specific details that only come from doing the thing
- [ ] Real examples and case studies with results

### Expertise
- [ ] Author named on the page, with a bio and credentials relevant to the topic
- [ ] Depth matches the topic's complexity (YMYL topics need more)
- [ ] Accurate, current information with correct terminology
- [ ] Claims are sourced, ideally to primary sources

### Authoritativeness
- [ ] The site or author is recognized in its niche (mentions, citations, links from relevant sites)
- [ ] The author has published elsewhere on the topic
- [ ] Others cite the content
- [ ] The organization holds relevant credentials or certifications

### Trustworthiness
- [ ] HTTPS across the site
- [ ] Transparent ownership: About page, company details
- [ ] Contact information that is easy to find
- [ ] Privacy policy and terms
- [ ] No misleading claims, clickbait titles, or fake urgency
- [ ] Content reviewed and updated, with a visible "last updated" date where freshness matters

## YMYL topics

"Your Money or Your Life" topics can affect a person's health, financial stability, safety, or the welfare of society. They get the strictest quality bar:

- Health and medical information
- Financial advice and transactions
- Legal information
- News and civic information
- Safety-related information
- Large purchases

For YMYL advice pages:
- Show verifiable credentials for the author, or for a named reviewer ("Medically reviewed by [name, credential]").
- First-hand experience can support advice ("what living with this condition is like") but does not replace expertise for medical, financial, or legal instructions.
- Add the disclaimer the field expects ("educational, not financial advice"; "consult a professional") and state the jurisdiction for legal content.
- Cite primary sources (studies, regulators, statutes) and date them.

## What not to do

- **Fabricated credentials or authors.** A fake byline or an invented degree is a trust failure, and it is easy to disprove.
- **Credentials that don't match the topic.** A marketing manager's byline on a medical claim adds nothing.
- **Outbound links as borrowed authority.** Linking to authoritative sites supports your claims; it does not make your page authoritative. Recognition comes from others.
- **Updating the date without updating the content.** A new "last updated" date on unchanged content is a misleading claim.
