# Review Requests and Replies

Frameworks and templates for review replies and neutral review requests. Adapt every reply to the review in front of you: name the service or detail the customer mentioned, and fill each bracket only with facts the business confirms (warranty terms, policies, response times). The review-policy rules are in SKILL.md (Earn and Respond to Reviews).

## Rules for Every Reply

- Reply promptly and personally; the same text pasted under many reviews reads as automated to customers.
- Never argue, and never confirm or disclose customer details (names of other people, job details, medical or financial information).
- Keep offers of refunds, discounts, or service recovery out of public replies; arrange them privately.
- Write for the next customer reading the thread: calm, specific, and short.
- Skip keyword stuffing; a reply is not a place for service or city keywords.
- When emotions run high, draft the reply and send it later.

---

## Positive Reviews (4-5 Stars)

**Framework:** thank them → acknowledge the specific thing they mentioned → one sentence of what you stand for → invite them back. Sign with a name.

> Thank you, [Name]! We're glad the [service] went smoothly and that [detail they mentioned] helped. [One sentence about how you work, e.g. "We always confirm the price before starting."] We're here whenever you need us. — [Your name], [Business]

Variants:
- **Mentions a team member:** "Thank you for the kind words about [team member]; we'll pass them on."
- **Mentions speed:** "Glad we could get to you quickly; we keep room in the schedule for urgent jobs." (only if true)
- **Mentions price or value:** "We believe in confirming the price before any work starts, so there are no surprises."
- **Mentions a lasting result:** "Happy to hear everything is still working. [Warranty or follow-up terms, if any] are there for peace of mind."

---

## Mixed or Neutral Reviews (3 Stars)

**Framework:** thank them → acknowledge what went well and what didn't → say what you're doing about it → offer a direct contact.

> Thank you for the honest feedback, [Name]. We're glad [what went well], and we're sorry [what fell short]. [What you are changing, if anything concrete.] If you'd like to talk it through, please contact [name] at [phone/email]. — [Your name], [Business]

Variants:
- **Wait time:** acknowledge that waiting is frustrating; mention a real scheduling change only if one exists.
- **Price surprise:** explain the estimate policy in one sentence and offer to go through the invoice privately.

---

## Negative Reviews (1-2 Stars)

**Framework (never argue):** acknowledge their experience → apologize for falling short of what they expected → move the conversation to a private channel → sign with a name and contact. Don't restate the accusation in detail, and don't admit facts you haven't checked.

> We're sorry your experience didn't meet your expectations, [Name]. That isn't the standard we aim for. Please contact [name] at [phone/email] so we can understand what happened and put it right. — [Your name], [Business]

Variants:
- **Work didn't fix the problem:** "We're sorry the issue wasn't fully resolved. [Follow-up or warranty terms, if any.] Please call [phone] so we can arrange a follow-up visit."
- **Staff conduct:** "This isn't how we expect our team to behave. We take it seriously and would like to speak with you directly at [phone/email]."
- **Pricing dispute:** "We're sorry the cost was unexpected. We'd welcome the chance to go through the details with you at [phone]."
- **Missed or late appointment:** "We apologize for the scheduling problem; we know you set aside time for us. Please contact [phone/email] and we'll make it a priority."
- **Damage claim:** "We take this seriously. Please contact [phone/email] so we can review it with you right away." (Involve insurance or legal advice before saying more.)
- **No record of the customer:** "We can't find a visit matching this description; it may be meant for another business. If we did serve you, please call [phone] so we can help." Report clear policy violations through the platform rather than in the reply.

## Review Requests

Send to every customer with a completed job or visit, not only the ones you expect to be happy. Use the correct location's review link. No incentives, no requested rating, no required wording.

**Short message (text or chat):**
> Hi [Name], thanks for choosing [Business] today. If you'd like to share your experience, you can leave a review here: [location review link]. We welcome honest feedback. — [Your name]

**Email:**
> Subject: How did your [service] go?
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thank you for choosing [Business] for your [service]. If you have a moment, we'd welcome an honest review of your experience: [location review link]
>
> If anything isn't right, please reply or call [phone] and we'll help.
>
> [Your name], [Business]

Check each channel's consent rules before sending (see the **sms** and **emails** skills).

## Length and Tone by Rating

| Rating | Length | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| 5 stars | 2-3 sentences | Warm, brief, personal |
| 4 stars | 2-3 sentences | Grateful; acknowledge any small issue |
| 3 stars | 3-4 sentences | Appreciative; say what you'll improve |
| 1-2 stars | 3-5 sentences | Calm, apologetic, move to private contact |
