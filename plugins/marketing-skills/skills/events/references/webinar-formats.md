# Webinar Formats

Pick the format that matches the goal and the audience's stage of awareness. The format sets the structure, the promotion angle, and the live-to-close. The funnel around every format is in [webinar-funnel.md](webinar-funnel.md).

## Format Selector

| Goal | Best format(s) | Why |
|------|----------------|-----|
| Net-new lead gen (cold) | Educational training, masterclass | Value first builds trust with people who don't know you |
| Pipeline acceleration | Product demo, customer story | Shows the solution working for people already evaluating |
| Product adoption / retention | Live training, office hours | Helps existing users get more value |
| Authority / brand | Expert panel, fireside chat | Borrowed credibility, shareable, low pitch |
| Demand at scale | Virtual summit (multi-session) | Many speakers = many promoters = reach |

## Format Details

**Educational training / masterclass**: teach a genuinely useful skill or framework. The offer is "do this faster or better with us." Highest trust; works for cold audiences. Risk: teaching so much there's no reason to buy. Leave a clear "do it with us" gap.

**Product demo**: show the product solving a real problem in a realistic scenario, not a feature tour. Best for warm pipeline. Risk: feature-dumping. Anchor every feature to a pain.

**Customer story / case study**: a customer tells how they got a result. Peer proof persuades evaluators more than vendor claims. Risk: too much backstory, not enough transferable insight.

**Expert panel**: 3–4 voices on a timely topic. Good reach (panelists promote) and authority. Lower direct conversion; pair it with a strong follow-up. Risk: meandering. A firm moderator is essential.

**Fireside chat**: one notable guest, conversational. Draws registrations on the guest's name. Low pitch, brand-building. Risk: no clear next step. Design the CTA deliberately.

**Office hours / AMA**: recurring live Q&A. Good for adoption and community, at low production cost. Risk: dead air. Seed a few questions.

**Virtual summit**: multi-session, multi-speaker event over hours or days. Maximum reach and list growth; high production effort. Risk: low per-session attendance. Design for on-demand viewing. Partner mechanics: **co-marketing**.

## Structure Templates

### Educational webinar (45 min)

1. **0–3 min, hook**: the promise, who it's for, the agenda, one credibility marker, and the note that an offer comes at the end.
2. **3–8 min, frame the problem**: make the cost of the status quo felt.
3. **8–30 min, teach**: 3 main points, an interaction every 5–10 minutes, at least one quick win they can use immediately.
4. **30–38 min, live-to-close**: recap the transformation, make the scripted transition, present one time-bound offer.
5. **38–45 min, Q&A**: answer objections in the open; restate the CTA at the end.

### Product demo (30 min)

1. 0–3 min: the problem and who has it.
2. 3–20 min: the product solving it in a realistic scenario; anchor each capability to a pain.
3. 20–25 min: proof (a customer result), then the offer or next step.
4. 25–30 min: Q&A and the CTA.

### Panel (45–60 min)

1. 0–5 min: the moderator frames the topic and introduces panelists.
2. 5–45 min: 4–6 prepared questions, with audience questions woven in.
3. Final 10 min: each panelist's one takeaway, then the host's single CTA.

Keep cold-audience webinars at or under about 60 minutes.
