# Video Strategy: Channels, Long-Form Scripts, and Repurposing

Use this file before production, when the user needs to decide *what* video to make: a YouTube channel or video series from zero, a long-form script, or a pipeline that turns existing content into video. Production tools are in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md). Short-form posting strategy, hooks, and the short-form script template are in the **social** skill (`references/short-form-video.md`).

Video earns trust that text rarely matches: a viewer who watches ten minutes of you working through their problem knows how you think. Build for depth first and distribution second.

## Pick the Mode

| Situation | Mode | Start at |
|---|---|---|
| No video presence yet | Strategy and channel setup | [Channel Setup](#channel-setup) |
| Strategy exists; videos need writing | Script | [Long-Form Script Structure](#long-form-script-structure) |
| Long-form assets exist (posts, podcasts, webinars, demos) | Repurpose and distribute | [Repurposing Pipeline](#repurposing-pipeline) |

Questions for strategy work, asked in one batch:

1. **Current state**: any video today (channel, social clips, webinar recordings)? Which content assets exist? Who makes it (solo, or a team with an editor)?
2. **Goal**: search and discovery, authority, lead gen, or product education? Primary platform? A cadence the team can actually hold?
3. **Audience and gap**: who is the ideal viewer (role, pain, sophistication)? What do competitors already do well on video, and where is the gap? (**youtube-full** can pull competitor channels and transcripts.)

## Channel Setup

### 1. Niche and Positioning

Broad channels compete with everyone. "Marketing" competes with every marketing channel; "email marketing for B2B SaaS founders with under 50 employees" can own its niche.

**Niche test:** describe the ideal subscriber in one sentence. If you can't, the niche is too broad.

| Dimension | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Which specific audience? | "Early-stage SaaS founders" |
| What problem | Which pain do they have? | "Can't afford a marketing team" |
| What you provide | What is your point of view? | "Low-budget growth tactics, tested" |
| Why you | Why should they believe you? | "Built two products to $1M ARR solo" |

### 2. Content Pillars

Define 3–4 recurring topic categories; every video maps to one. Pillars give subscribers a reason to expect the next video and keep the channel's topic signal coherent. Example for a B2B marketing channel:

1. **How-to tutorials**: step-by-step implementation (largest search demand)
2. **Tool reviews and comparisons**: evaluation content (commercial intent)
3. **Case studies and teardowns**: authority (highest trust)
4. **Opinion and contrarian takes**: shareable, comment-driving

### 3. Keyword Targets

YouTube is a search engine: treat each video as an answer to a query people type.

| Query type | Pattern | Volume | Competition | Funnel stage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Informational | "how to," "what is," "tutorial" | High | High | Discovery |
| Comparative | "X vs Y," "best X for Y" | Medium | Medium | Evaluation, commercial intent |
| Problem-specific | "why isn't X working," "fix X" | Lower | Lower | High intent |

Target one primary keyword per video. Put it in the title (within the first ~60 characters, before truncation), the first two sentences of the description, and say it aloud in the first 30 seconds (captions and transcripts are indexed). Tags carry little weight; don't spend time on them. New channels win on specific, long-tail queries; "marketing tips" is a graveyard. For how titles, chapters, and transcripts get a video cited in AI answers, see **ai-seo** (`references/youtube-ai-citations.md`).

### 4. 90-Day Launch Plan

| Weeks | Focus | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Channel setup; first 3 videos scripted | Channel art, banner, trailer, videos 1–3 ready |
| 3–6 | Consistency: publish 1–2 per week | 8–12 published videos |
| 7–10 | Double down on what retains | 2–3 videos built from retention data on the best performers |
| 11–13 | Cut top videos into Shorts | 10+ Shorts that send viewers to the channel |

Pick a cadence the team can sustain for the full 90 days. One video a week, every week, beats bursts followed by silence: the audience learns when to expect you, and you get enough data to learn what works.

## Long-Form Script Structure

**Hook (0–30 seconds).** Most viewers decide here whether to stay; check the retention curve of your own videos for where the drop happens. Hook types:

- Problem statement: "If your email open rates are below 20%, here's exactly why."
- Counterintuitive claim: "The biggest mistake B2B marketers make is posting too much."
- Result promise: "I'll show you the 3-step system we used to triple demo requests."

Open on the hook itself, before any logo sting or long intro.

**Context (30–90 seconds).** Why this matters, who it's for, what they will walk away with.

**Body (most of the runtime).** For each major point: problem → solution → example → result. Plan B-roll and on-screen text per point. Add chapters to long videos: timestamps in the description, the first at `0:00`, at least three, each at least 10 seconds long. Chapters help viewers navigate and can show in search results.

**CTA (final 60 seconds).** One explicit action: subscribe, get the resource, book a demo, or watch the next video. A buried or missing CTA wastes the trust the video earned.

## Repurposing Pipeline

One long-form source (a post, podcast, webinar, or demo) becomes:

- 1 full YouTube video, where the source supports it
- 3–5 short clips of the key moments and quotable insights
- Platform-adapted versions: YouTube Shorts (searchable title), Instagram Reels (hook first, heavy captions), LinkedIn video (professional framing, text overlay), TikTok (native feel, trend-aware)

**Blog to video:**

| Blog element | Video equivalent |
|---|---|
| H2 headings | Chapters / timestamps |
| Key stats and quotes | Pull quotes for B-roll overlays |
| Step-by-step sections | Tutorial segments |
| Conclusion / summary | Short-form clip |

**Workflow:**

1. **Pick the source**: the post, episode, or webinar with the most traffic or engagement.
2. **Extract the hook**: the single most compelling insight or result.
3. **Write the short script**: one idea, under 60 seconds (template in **social** `references/short-form-video.md`).
4. **Adapt per platform**: same core, re-edited for 9:16 with captions, a native hook, and a platform-specific CTA. Horizontal video dropped unchanged into vertical feeds underperforms.
5. **Stagger the release** rather than publishing the same clip everywhere on the same day.

The production chain for the clips (transcript editing, auto-clipping, captions) is in SKILL.md, Editing & Repurposing Tools.

## Flags to Raise Unprompted

- A script with no explicit hook in the opening seconds: review the hook before production.
- Keyword targets too generic to rank for a new channel.
- A cadence the team can't sustain.
- Long videos without chapters.
- No CTA, or a CTA buried mid-video.
- Repurposing without platform adaptation (wrong aspect ratio, no captions).
- No defined ideal viewer: generic content builds no loyal audience.
- More than one idea in a short-form clip.
