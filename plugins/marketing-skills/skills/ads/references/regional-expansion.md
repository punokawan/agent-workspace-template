# Taking Paid Acquisition to a New Region

Geography is the high-budget, high-effort lever in the scaling quadrant ([b2b-paid-playbook.md](b2b-paid-playbook.md)). Load this before launching paid campaigns in a new country, region, or language.

## Gate: expand only when the current market earns it

Add budget for a new region only when all three hold:

- **Unit economics hold at home.** CAC or CPA has stayed under target for several consecutive weeks (four is a common bar) and passes the payback gate in [payback-period.md](payback-period.md).
- **Lead quality holds.** The qualified-lead rate is at or above the current market's own baseline, not just cheap volume.
- **Someone can work the leads.** Sales or support covers the region's language and business hours.

Run the new region as its own campaigns, with its own targets and its own learning phase. Expect higher early CPA; judge the region on its own trend, never against the home market's mature numbers.

## Localize the campaign, not just the words

- Adapt the ad copy, offer, and proof for the market. A literal translation keeps the home market's idioms and objections.
- Build regional landing pages that continue the ad: language, currency and price format, date and number formats, local customer proof, local support hours.
- Carry the region in campaign names and UTMs, add a region dimension to reporting, and route regional leads in the CRM.
- Schedule delivery and follow-up for the region's time zone and sales hours.
- Have a native speaker review every ad and page before launch.
- Multilingual search pages (hreflang, regional search consoles) belong to the **seo-audit** skill.

## Legal and consent checks

Verify each region's rules before launch. These are starting points, not legal advice:

- **EU/EEA and UK:** consent for analytics and advertising cookies, consent mode for the ad platforms (the **conversion-tracking** skill covers the failure modes), consent for marketing email.
- **Canada:** privacy law for personal data and anti-spam law for commercial email; Quebec requires French in commercial advertising.
- **Everywhere:** privacy policy and terms in the region's language where required, and the platform's country-specific policies for regulated categories (health, finance, legal).

## Messaging differences

Tone, formality, and the proof that persuades differ between markets. Treat each difference as a hypothesis to test in the region, not an assumption: pull the region's own customer language from reviews and sales calls before writing, and test the angle before scaling the spend.
