# Copy Frameworks: Choosing the Structure of an Ad

Frameworks are starting structures, not forms to fill in. Pick one per concept from the angle and the reader's stage, then write it in the customer's own words from the grounded inputs corpus (SKILL.md, Grounded Inputs). Every example below is an invented illustration with placeholder facts: replace each bracket with the user's verified material, and run the No AI Tells check on the result.

The PAS, BAB, social-proof-lead, feature-benefit-bridge, and direct-response templates, the search and social headline formulas, and the CTA lists live in the **ads** skill's ad copy templates reference. This file adds the frameworks that reference lacks and the rules for choosing between them.

## One ad, one job

Every ad has one job: get the right person to stop, read, and take one action. An ad that tries to do three things does none of them well. One message, one CTA, one next step.

## Choose by funnel stage

| Stage | Goal | Frameworks |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness (cold) | Interrupt, be relevant, open a question | PAS, contrarian, specificity |
| Consideration | Educate, differentiate, build trust | AIDA, FAB, how-it-works, BAB |
| Decision (warm, retargeting) | Prove, remove risk, ask | Social proof, BAB, specificity with a guarantee |
| Retention and upsell | Remind of value, expand use | BAB, feature highlight, usage milestone |

The stage is a hypothesis about the audience. When unsure, state the assumption and test two stages (see [awareness-headlines.md](awareness-headlines.md)).

## Choose by platform

| Platform | Frameworks | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Google search ads | Specificity, benefit-first | The searcher already stated the need |
| Meta feed, cold | PAS, contrarian | Interrupt and engage fast |
| Meta feed, retargeting | BAB, social proof | They know you; sell the outcome |
| LinkedIn | AIDA, FAB, how-it-works | Longer attention in a work context |
| TikTok | Compressed PAS, hook-first | The first three seconds decide it |
| X | Contrarian, specificity | Opinionated, concrete posts travel |

## Choose by product type

| Product | Usual starting framework |
|---|---|
| SaaS or B2B tool that removes a specific frustration | PAS |
| Coaching, course, or other transformation | BAB |
| Ecommerce or feature-rich physical product | FAB |
| Content or information product | AIDA |
| App or tool launch, direct response | 4P |
| Service, consulting, personal brand | Star-story-solution |

## The frameworks

### AIDA: attention, interest, desire, action

**Best for:** video scripts, longer LinkedIn copy, awareness campaigns.

```
Attention: the hook (first three seconds or first sentence)
Interest:  why this matters to this reader specifically
Desire:    the outcome they want, with proof
Action:    one clear CTA
```

Video outline (placeholders):
```
[0-3s]   "Your team spends [X hours] a week on reports nobody opens."
[3-10s]  "For a [role], that is [X hours] a month taken from campaign work."
[10-20s] "[Product] builds the report from your connected tools every Monday."
[20-25s] "Start the [N]-day trial. No card needed."
```

### FAB: feature, advantage, benefit

**Best for:** decision stage, retargeting product-page visitors, feature-rich products.

```
Feature:   what it is or does
Advantage: why that beats the alternative
Benefit:   what the customer gains in their work or life
```

The common mistake is stopping at the feature. "Two-factor authentication" is a feature; "bank-level security" is an advantage; "your customer records stay protected" is the benefit.

### 4P: promise, picture, proof, push

**Best for:** app and tool launches, direct response.

```
Promise: one bold, true claim (the headline)
Picture: a vivid scene of life with the product
Proof:   a number, a review, a named customer
Push:    the CTA, with a real reason to act now
```

### Star, story, solution

**Best for:** services, consulting, founder-led brands.

```
Star:     the customer is the hero, not the brand
Story:    their struggle, told the way they would tell it
Solution: how the service changed their situation
```

### Contrarian frame

**Best for:** awareness in a saturated category where every ad says the same thing.

Challenge a belief the category takes for granted, then give your reasoning and your different approach.

```
Headline: "Sending more email won't fix your open rate"
Body:     "[Product] finds the [N] segments that still open and writes to them first.
           Fewer sends, more replies."
```

It needs substance behind it. If the product is "like the others, but better," use another frame.

### Specificity frame

**Best for:** every stage. The most underused frame.

Specific claims beat vague ones on attention and on trust. Upgrade every vague line:

| Vague | Specific (with verified numbers) |
|---|---|
| "Save time on reporting" | "Cut reporting from [X hours] to [Y minutes]" |
| "Trusted by leading companies" | "Used by [N] growth teams in [N] countries" |
| "Easy to use" | "Set up in [N] minutes, without an engineer" |
| "Get better results" | "Customers see [X]% more trial-to-paid within [N] days" |

No number yet? Ask five customers for their before and after. One real number beats ten adjectives. Numbers need evidence; a review supports the pain, not a performance claim (see [awareness-headlines.md](awareness-headlines.md), Gather Proof Before Copy).

### How-it-works frame

**Best for:** consideration-stage audiences who are curious but not convinced.

Show the mechanism, so the result stops sounding like magic:

```
Headline: "How [Product] works, in three steps"
Body:     "1. Connect your tools ([N] minutes, no code)
           2. Set a rule: when a lead scores over [N], alert sales
           3. Leads get a reply in minutes, around the clock"
CTA:      "See it run on your data"
```

## More headline patterns

These complement the angle categories in SKILL.md:

| Pattern | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| How-to | "How [persona] [achieves outcome]" | "How growth teams cut CAC by [X]% in one quarter" |
| Number-led | "[Number] [thing] [result]" | "[N] days. One setup. Every report automated." |
| Objection-led | Answer the main reason they don't buy | "You can set this up without an engineer" |
| Direct comparison | Against their current approach | "Cheaper than [enterprise tool], more powerful than your spreadsheet" |

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Why it fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "We are the #1 platform for..." | Unsubstantiated, ignored, often disapproved | Lead with proof, not a ranking |
| "Solutions for modern teams" | Says nothing; every team is modern | Name the team and the problem |
| "Powerful yet easy to use" | Every product says it | Show the result instead of describing the product |
| "Unlock your potential" | No content at all | Name the specific outcome |
| "Join thousands of happy customers" | Vague and dated | "[N] companies use [Product] to [outcome]" |
| Several emojis | Desperate on LinkedIn, clutter on Google | One at most, and only if it carries meaning |
