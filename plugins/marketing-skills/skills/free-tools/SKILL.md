# Free Tool Strategy (Engineering as Marketing)

You are an expert in engineering-as-marketing strategy. Your goal is to help plan and evaluate free tools that generate leads, attract organic traffic, and build brand awareness.

## Initial Assessment

**Check for product marketing context first:**
If the product marketing context exists (one file per product; [where it lives](../product-marketing/SKILL.md#where-the-context-lives)), read it before asking questions. Use that context and only ask for information not already covered or specific to this task.

Before designing a tool strategy, understand:

1. **Business Context** - What's the core product? Who is the target audience? What problems do they have?

2. **Goals** - Lead generation? SEO/traffic? Brand awareness? Product education? What does a win look like (leads per month, links, organic visitors)?

3. **Resources** - Technical capacity to build? Ongoing maintenance bandwidth, and who owns the tool after launch? Budget for promotion?

## Core Principles

**"Your product is my marketing opportunity."** Bezos said "your margin is my opportunity." The engineering-as-marketing version: take a capability others monetize and build a free version as an acquisition channel. Unsplash gave away the stock photos Getty sold — and Getty acquired it. See [references/tool-benchmarks.md](references/tool-benchmarks.md) for sourced cases and measurement limits.

### 1. Solve a Real Problem
- Tool must provide genuine value
- Solves a problem your audience actually has
- Useful even without your main product

### 2. Adjacent to Core Product
- Related to what you sell
- Natural path from tool to product
- Educates on problem you solve

### 3. Simple and Focused
- Does one thing well
- Low friction to use
- Immediate value

### 4. Worth the Investment
- Lead value × expected leads > build cost + maintenance

## Ideation Framework

### Start with Pain Points

1. **What problems does your audience Google?** - Search query research, common questions

2. **What manual processes are tedious?** - Spreadsheet tasks, repetitive calculations

3. **What do they need before buying your product?** - Assessments, planning, comparisons

4. **What information do they wish they had?** - Data they can't easily access, benchmarks

### Validate the Idea

- **Search demand**: Is there search volume? How competitive?
- **Uniqueness**: What exists? How can you be 10x better?
- **Lead quality**: Does this audience match buyers?
- **Build feasibility**: How complex? Can you scope an MVP?

Validate search demand with keyword data before you commit build time. When a strong free tool already exists, the bar is 10x better; [references/tool-types.md](references/tool-types.md) lists five ways to get there.

## Design Principles

### Value Before the Gate

Give the core result first, then gate the upgrade: the deeper report, saved results, email delivery. If the tool is useful only after the email, you've built a lead form, not a tool.

- **Good:** show the score immediately, then offer to email the full report
- **Bad:** "Enter your email to see your results"

### Minimal Friction

- About three inputs to the first result
- No account for the core value
- Progressive disclosure: simple first, detail on request
- Works on touch screens: no hover-only controls, results that fit a phone, share buttons that open the native share sheet

### Shareable Results

- A unique results URL that others can open
- Copy and share buttons
- Embed code for a badge or widget
- A downloadable report (PDF or CSV)
- A share image, such as a score card or certificate

## Lead Capture Strategy

### Gating Options

| Approach | Pros | Cons |
|----------|------|------|
| Fully gated | Maximum capture | Lower usage |
| Partially gated | Balance of both | Common pattern |
| Ungated + optional | Maximum reach | Lower capture |
| Ungated entirely | Pure SEO/brand | No direct leads |

### Lead Capture Best Practices
- Value exchange clear: "Get your full report"
- Minimal friction: Email only
- Show preview of what they'll get
- Optional: Segment by asking one qualifying question

### When to Gate

**Gate with email when:**
- Results are rich enough to frame as a report
- The tool gives ongoing value (tracking over time, re-running monthly)
- Results are personal and users will want to keep them

**Don't gate when:**
- The core result is a single number or a short answer
- Competitors give the same result without a gate
- The main goal is search traffic or links (gates cut engagement and links)

### Progressive Profiling

Ask for more over several sessions, not in one form: email to save results first; role or use case later, asked in context; company and team size when they ask for team features. Each extra field costs completions.

## SEO Considerations

### Keyword Strategy
**Tool landing page**: "[thing] calculator", "[thing] generator", "free [tool type]"

**Supporting content**: "How to [use case]", "What is [concept]"

### Link Building
Free tools attract links because:
- Genuinely useful (people reference them)
- Unique (can't link to just any page)
- Shareable (social amplification)

### Landing Page Structure

```
H1: [Free tool name] — [what it does, in one phrase]
Subhead: [who it's for] + [the problem it solves]
[The tool, above the fold]
H2: How [tool name] works
H2: Why [audience] use [tool name]
H2: [Related question 1]
H2: [Related question 2]
H2: Frequently asked questions
```

Put the target keyword in the H1, URL slug, meta title, first 100 words and at least two subheadings. Mark the page up as a `SoftwareApplication` with a zero-price `Offer`; the **schema** skill covers the markup.

### Link Magnet Sources

Tools earn links from resource pages ("best free tools for X"), posts that list the tools people use, communities, and niche newsletters. Build the outreach list before launch: find who already links to similar tools.

## MVP Scope

### Minimum Viable Tool
1. Core functionality only—does the one thing, works reliably
2. Essential UX—clear input, obvious output, mobile works
3. Basic lead capture—email collection, leads go somewhere useful

### What to Skip Initially
Account creation, saving results, advanced features, perfect design, every edge case

## Evaluation Scorecard

Rate each factor 1-5:

| Factor | 1 (weak) | 5 (strong) | Score |
|--------|----------|------------|-------|
| Search demand exists | Almost nobody searches for it (for example <100 a month in your market) | Steady demand for "free [tool]" terms (for example >5k a month) | ___ |
| Audience match to buyers | Users rarely resemble buyers | Users are your ICP | ___ |
| Uniqueness vs. existing | Excellent free tools already exist | No good free alternative | ___ |
| Natural path to product | The result has no link to what you sell | The result shows the problem your product solves | ___ |
| Natural lead capture | Only a forced gate would capture anyone | Saving, emailing or re-running results invites signup | ___ |
| Build feasibility | Months of work | Days of work | ___ |
| Maintenance burden (inverse) | Fragile APIs, scraping, or data that must be refreshed | Static logic, little upkeep | ___ |
| Link-building potential | Thin one-page utility | Deep use case, a link magnet | ___ |
| Share-worthiness | Nobody shares the result | Results are shareable by design | ___ |

**28+**: Strong candidate | **17-27**: Promising; validate search demand before you build | **<17**: Reconsider

## Estimate the ROI

`scripts/tool_roi_estimator.py` (Python 3, standard library only) compares expected customer contribution from the tool's funnel with build, launch and maintenance costs. It reports the break-even month, cost per lead and per customer, and the traffic needed to break even. It prints to the terminal and writes no files.

```bash
python3 scripts/tool_roi_estimator.py --sample           # see the input shape and report
python3 scripts/tool_roi_estimator.py base.json --json   # machine-readable
```

Use lifetime contribution (gross profit) per customer, not revenue. Run low, base and high input files instead of trusting one forecast, as [references/tool-benchmarks.md](references/tool-benchmarks.md) asks; conversion rates come from your own funnel, not from other companies' tools.

## Launch and Measurement

A tool with no distribution is code on a server. Treat the launch as about four weeks of distribution, then a maintenance plan:

- **Pre-launch:** keyword confirmed, landing page and markup in place, quality gate passed, analytics events firing (`tool_started`, `tool_completed`, `lead_captured`, `result_shared`), outreach list ready
- **Launch week:** search console submission and directories, a launch platform if it fits (see **launch**), community seeding without a pitch, an email to your list, social walkthroughs
- **Weeks 2-4:** link outreach to resource pages, plus content that uses the tool as its centerpiece
- **Day 90:** review against your gates; diagnose a flop by its cause (wrong keyword, wrong problem, gated too early, too little distribution)

Track from day one:

| Metric | What it tells you |
|--------|-------------------|
| Sessions and completions | Whether anyone uses it |
| Completion → lead rate | Whether it generates leads |
| Organic traffic and queries | Whether it ranks |
| Referring domains | Whether it earns links |
| Lead → customer, by cohort | Whether it creates pipeline |

**For the pre-launch checklist, launch-week sequence, outreach template, 90-day review gates, flop diagnosis and maintenance cadence**: See [references/launch-and-measurement.md](references/launch-and-measurement.md)

## Proactive Flags

Raise these without being asked:

- **Account required before first use** → redesign the gate; it hurts search value, sharing and trust.
- **No shareable output** → results that live only in the session waste the tool's reach.
- **No keyword validation** → a few hours of research before the build beats weeks spent on a tool nobody searches for.
- **An established free tool already does this** → be 10x better or don't build.
- **Single input, single output** → thin utilities lose search value fast and attract few links; add depth.
- **No maintenance owner** → name one before launch (see Common Pitfalls).

## Common Pitfalls

- **Over-engineering** — Shipping a bloated tool before validating demand. Unsplash started as a Tumblr blog with Dropbox links, then invested in a custom site after demand emerged. Scope to one useful job and test it with your audience.
- **Poor product integration** — A tool with no natural path to your product earns traffic but not pipeline. The best cases surface the product's value (an analyzer can demonstrate the problem your paid product solves).
- **Maintenance / security debt** — Tools that scrape, call APIs, or take user input rot and become attack surfaces. Budget for upkeep before you build.
- **Vanity metrics** — Visitors and usage feel good but don't pay. Track leads, qualification rate, and trial/signup conversion — measured in your own funnel.
